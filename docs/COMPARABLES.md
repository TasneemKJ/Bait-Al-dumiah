### What we take

1. **The house answers absence with a trace, never with loss** (from Neko Atsume, made ours). When the player comes back, something shows that the house lived while they were away: a doll moved to the window, a crumb on the stair where the shy visitor stood. Evidence of a visit you did not see is warmer than any notice, and it fits our no-offline-decay rule.
2. **Cute is how we earn the right to the creepy** (from Cult of the Lamb, made ours). The dolls must be loved before the dark asks anything of them: each of the three gets one small habit the player notices before the first night that unsettles them. The creepy then lands on someone, not on a set.
3. **One rule changes after dark** (from Dredge, made ours). The same miniature should read differently at night by one clear rule (for example, what the lamp does not reach is not safe to leave a doll in), not by more decoration. And from Tiny Glade's complaint: every price must sit on content the player can already see in the house.

## 2026-11-16 (round 2): five puzzle-box and hand-made mystery games

### The Room (Fireproof Games, 2012, with sequels)
**Loop:** one object at a time. Turn it, zoom into it, and find the drawer, dial or seam that opens the next layer. The touch is the puzzle.
**Art:** heavy wood, brass and candlelight in a dark room. The atmosphere comes from the light and the sound of the mechanisms.
**Story and onboarding:** almost no words. Letters drip-feed a strange story, and later reviewers say the sequels added little to it.
**Players:** praise the clever, tactile puzzles and the mood. Some find the later games "more of the same".
**Earns:** premium. The first game was $0.99 on iPhone (Pocket), Old Sins and The Room Three $4.99. The series sold more than 6.5 million copies, and the iPad version broke even in its first week (Wikipedia, older figures).

### Behind the Frame: The Finest Scenery (Silver Lining Studio, 2021)
**Loop:** paint, find the missing pieces of a picture in a small flat, and watch the neighbour across the way.
**Art:** soft, Ghibli-like painting. Reviewers call the visuals its biggest asset.
**Story and onboarding:** quiet daily routines that turn into an unexpected twist.
**Players:** praise the art, the story and the calm. They complain it is about an hour long, with little reason to replay; one calls $12.99 too much for that length.
**Earns:** premium on PC, consoles and mobile.

### Gorogoa (Jason Roberts / Annapurna, 2017)
**Loop:** four hand-drawn panels. The player zooms, slides and stacks pictures so one scene opens into another.
**Art:** thousands of hand-drawn illustrations. Apple named it iPad Game of the Year 2018. App Store 4.6 from 2.3K ratings.
**Story and onboarding:** wordless. The player learns by doing.
**Players:** love its originality and how satisfying the pieces feel. Complaints: short (under 2 hours), a loose story some found confusing, and a price some thought high for the length.
**Earns:** $4.99 on iOS, $14.99 on PC and Switch.

### Spirittea (No Gravity, 2023)
**Loop:** run a bathhouse for spirits, find the ones causing mischief in town, and get to know the villagers.
**Art:** pixel art with a warm town and odd spirit guests.
**Story and onboarding:** cosy and funny, with a grumpy spirit guide players remember.
**Players:** find the bathhouse loop "immensely satisfying". They complain the bath chores repeat and the map is thin.
**Earns:** premium (price not verified).

### Little Misfortune (Killmonday, 2019)
**Loop:** walk a little girl through a short adventure led by a voice in her head. Choices, little play.
**Art:** a sweet storybook look over something disturbing.
**Story and onboarding:** cute on the surface, with family neglect underneath that shows "only now and then".
**Players:** praise the humour, the voice work and the mood ("charming… and deeply depressing at times"). They complain there is "nothing to do besides watch" and that it lasts 3–4 hours.
**Earns:** premium, about $15 on PC (not verified).

## What we take (round 2, principles made our own)
1. **Every object is a little box with a secret** (The Room, Gorogoa). The dollhouse already holds objects; the best puzzle-box games make the touch itself the discovery: a drawer that sticks, a panel that slides, a music box that opens to a second floor. Bait's next rooms should hide one secret in a mechanism the player handles, not behind a button.
2. **Cute over sadness needs something to do** (Little Misfortune, Behind the Frame). Players praise the mood and fault the passive watching. Bait's sadness, the three little souls, must come out through the player's own hands: what they mend, find or give. Never a scene the player only watches.
3. **Short and hand-made is loved; short and dear is not** (Gorogoa, Behind the Frame). Length matters less than the felt price. Bait's Full House purchase should show its rooms in play first, and the store page should say plainly how long the house takes.
